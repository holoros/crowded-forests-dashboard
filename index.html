From remeasured Forest Inventory and Analysis plots. Crowded means a stand holds at least two thirds of the trees its site can support; open means under one third, measured over the area the stand occupies on its inventory plot, as in the October 26 preprint. Counts are all remeasured conditions and plots in the state; the most common cause is the agent recorded most often on its damaged conditions. The published 6% of forest land past the 60% line averages crowding over larger areas, so the shares are not comparable. Preliminary, not yet peer reviewed.

Nationwide

Share of stands damaged between inventory visits, by how crowded they were at the first visit, condition area basis, 148,604 forest conditions. The preprint's model, which allows for stand size and height, puts the chance of damage at a shallow peak a little under half full, four to eight points above either end, with the direction differing among ecoregions. What crowding changes consistently is the cause: the insect share rises 7 to 12 points and the fire share falls 5 to 12.

What this means for carbon

The state figures above describe how often forests are damaged. Their carbon consequences are known nationally and regionally, not yet by state. These are the published findings the article draws on.

FindingWhat it saysSource

The preprint's supplemental carbon factorial puts disturbance at least on par with harvest as a drain on U.S. forest carbon over the coming century under a no-harvest scenario. That result depends on the accounting convention and is not resolved by state.
