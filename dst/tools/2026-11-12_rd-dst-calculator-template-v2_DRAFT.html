The map pulls toward the middle: sparse stands read 0.20 to 0.30 high, dense stands 0.15 to 0.17 low. The calculator gives no status or target from a map value.

Relative density = SDI / SDImax, SDI = Σ ni(Di/25.4)1.605 ha⁻¹. The default SDImax is the median for the forest type group in the region; plot values typically run from about 45% below to 50% above it (60% below to 84% above for spruce/fir), so enter a stand value when you have one; the range under the relative density shows the effect.

Mortality flag. Mortality on intervals without natural disturbance does not rise steadily with crowding at fixed top height, against self-thinning expectations; where it peaks differs by region. It is shown as observed and no mortality cost is credited above the band. Natural disturbance here means insects, disease, fire, animals and weather (FIA codes 10 to 54).

Net growth uses the forest type group's own curve and height-specific tests where the type has at least 6,000 intervals in the region, and the regional curve otherwise; the label under the band says which. Gross growth, mortality and disturbance are regional. The type group also sets the default SDImax and height.

Thinning trials: in the CFRU Commercial Thinning Research Network, survivor basal area growth in the North band exceeded the class below it by 0.16 m² ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (0.03 to 0.29), so the North floor holds there; above the band the trial could not tell (it detects differences of about 0.19). In New Brunswick partial cut plots denser classes grew more, and verdicts change with SDImax. Targets are provisional, and curves come from plots with no harvest between visits, not thinning responses.
