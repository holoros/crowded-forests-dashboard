Relative density decision support tool: methods and status (DRAFT, 13 November 2026)

Released 13 November 2026. Revises the 12 November version 2 (2026-11-12_rd-dst-methods_DRAFT.md, unchanged on disk) after the 13 November release review, which traced 258 numbers to DATA files. Every number traces to a file listed in the last section. The content inventory at the end lists what changed in version 2 and in this revision.

Purpose and decisions

The tool supports strategic and tactical forest management by placing stands and landscapes against a relative density (RD) band where growth is near its maximum, with mortality and disturbance hazard shown as the cost of sitting outside it. Decisions already made by Aaron: a band rather than a single optimum; strategic and tactical views built together; stand input from field data with a density map as a first guess; carbon (Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹) and merchantable volume (m³ ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹) outputs; the Maine 10 m map first; the floor at the regional leveling point minus 0.05; a ceiling only where the data show a cost; density related mortality shown as observed with a flag.

Data

The engine uses 276,984 remeasured intervals on 85,821 US Forest Service FIA plots with no harvest between visits, the retained set of the RD growth manuscript. Growth, mortality and net change are annualized interval totals in NSVB aboveground carbon and in net merchantable bole volume (VOLCFNET, trees at least 12.7 cm DBH). Each plot carries an EPA Level II ecoregion grouped into four regions (North, Central, Southeast, West), forest type group, a modeled plot SDIₘₐₓ, and top height (mean height of the largest 100 trees ha⁻¹). The natural disturbance flag was rebuilt for version 2 from the condition records of the visits inside each interval: insects, disease, fire, animals and weather (DSTRBCD 10 to 54) count as natural disturbance; vegetation and suppression (60) and unknown (70) now count with density related mortality. The rebuilt any-code flag reproduced the original flag on all 276,984 intervals; intervals flagged only for code 60 or 70 are under 1% of the total, and the natural disturbance share is 24.0%. No plot coordinates entered the engine; hexagon assignment for the strategic view ran on Cardinal and only aggregates left the server. The population is unharvested FIA plots, so shares are shares of plots, not of forest area, and plots cut or salvaged between visits are absent.

Engine

One crowding definition is used throughout: RD at the start of the interval, the value a manager measures before acting. Across plots, high RD goes with short, low biomass stands, so a curve on RD alone mixes stand development with crowding, and every response is fitted as a tensor smooth te(RD, ln top height) by region (mgcv bam, fREML, intervals weighted by length). The prediction grid covers top heights of 4 to 30 m and RD of 0.05 to 1.00; a grid cell is reported only where at least 30 intervals fall within ±0.025 RD and ±10% top height.

Net growth is fitted directly and is the primary curve for every growth comparison. Alongside it, gross growth, mortality on intervals without natural disturbance, mortality on disturbed intervals and the probability of natural disturbance (complementary log log binomial with a ln(interval length) offset) are fitted. Expected mortality is split as density related mortality plus the disturbance probability over the region’s median interval length (North 7.0, Central 8.6, Southeast 7.7, West 10.1 yr) times the excess mortality on disturbed intervals, so damage is not counted twice. The bootstrap uses Gaussian mean fits for that split because the server was shared; a Tweedie split at the point estimate moves net growth by a median of 0.009 to 0.015 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in the North, Central and Southeast regions (maximum 0.15 at the edges of the data) and did not converge in the West. The split reproduces the direct net fit within 0.04 to 0.11 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ at the reference heights, which is why the direct fit, not the sum of parts, carries the decisions.

Uncertainty comes from a whole plot bootstrap within region, B = 200, with 95% percentile intervals on every curve and statistic. The ceiling and floor tests run inside each replicate: the net maximum is re-located in every replicate, the ceiling contrast is net growth 0.10 above the maximum minus net growth at the maximum, and the floor contrast is net growth at the floor minus net growth 0.10 below it. A ceiling is indicated only when the point estimate is testable, at least half of the replicates are testable, and the upper 95% bound of the contrast is below zero. The bootstrap does not cover the choice of smooth basis, the leveling points or the support rule. Row shuffling gave identical point estimates in all four regions; this is a check on determinism, not a validation.

Pre-flight check

Model form: smooths in RD and log height with no imposed shape. Extrapolation domain: the support rule, with top heights down to 4 m so short western stands are no longer dropped. Uncertainty: as above. Attenuation: observed on predicted slopes for gross carbon growth in five fold, plot blocked cross validation were 0.98 to 1.00 in version 1, but that covers gross growth only, and cross validated R² was 0.08 in the Southeast and at most 0.45 elsewhere, so individual stand predictions are weak. Biological realism fails in one place: at fixed top height, mortality without natural disturbance does not rise steadily with crowding; at the reference heights it is lower 0.20 above the band center than inside the band in the North (0.43 against 0.57 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹) and Central region (0.59 against 0.71), and where it peaks differs by region and forest type. That runs against self-thinning. It is shown as observed and no mortality cost is credited above the band.

Confounding and measurement error

Three checks were run on the direct net carbon curve, each with its own whole plot bootstrap (B = 100). Smooth adjustments are read at each region’s reference height; tercile and previous visit subsets are read at the subset’s own median top height, because the terciles differ in stand height. First, adding a smooth of ln SDIₘₐₓ to the model left the Central, Southeast and West growth peaks where they were (0.88, 0.99, 0.38) and moved the North peak to the edge of the data (0.98). Second, within SDIₘₐₓ terciles the peak depends strongly on the tercile: in the high tercile net growth peaks at 0.66 in the North, 0.60 in the Central region, 0.70 in the Southeast and 0.71 in the West, while in the low tercile it keeps rising to 0.98, 0.82 and 1.00 in the first three regions (0.26 in the West). Third, start RD was calibrated on the previous visit’s RD to reduce single visit measurement error (plots with at least three visits; first stage R² 0.33 to 0.61). With the calibrated axis the North peak moves from 0.98 to 0.64 and a ceiling is indicated (net growth 0.05 lower 0.10 above the peak, 0.01 to 0.21), and strong declines above the peak appear in the Southeast and West; in the Central region the point estimate cannot be tested, but the replicate interval for the decline is −0.88 to −0.47. Calibration compresses the RD range to about 0.3 to 0.8 and keeps only plots with repeated visits, so this is a sensitivity rather than a replacement. Taken together, the absence of a ceiling in the Central and Southeast data is not robust: it is largest where the modeled SDIₘₐₓ is low and it shrinks or disappears when RD noise is reduced, which is what one would expect if high single visit RD partly marks underestimated carrying capacity or measurement noise rather than true crowding. The tool therefore keeps those regions labeled “no tested ceiling” rather than “no ceiling”. Two further checks were run the same way. Site productivity: ESI (v6r, North America, 1 km) was extracted at public FIA plot coordinates, which are fuzzed by up to about 1 km and so match the raster scale; the public coordinate table on the server covers 66,326 of the 85,821 plots, and ESI correlates weakly with start RD (r from −0.15 to 0.16 by region). Adding a smooth of ESI changes little (peaks at 0.95, 0.88, 0.99 and 0.38). Within ESI terciles, ceilings appear on better sites: in the North the middle and upper terciles peak at 0.59 with net growth 0.03 and 0.01 lower 0.10 above the peak (upper bounds −0.02 and −0.01), the upper Southeast tercile peaks at 0.71 with a decline of 0.05 (0.03 to 0.32), and the West shows a ceiling in every tercile; the Central region stays untestable. Harvest selection: weighting each interval by the inverse probability of remaining unharvested (0.84 to 1.50, mean 1) leaves every regional result essentially unchanged (North peak 0.57, ceiling contrast −0.03; West −0.25). Site index therefore does not explain the Central and Southeast pattern on its own, harvest attrition does not move it, and the strongest signals for a hidden ceiling remain the SDIₘₐₓ and measurement error checks.

What the engine shows

Results are at each region’s median top height (North 18 m, Central 21 m, Southeast 21 m, West 16 m) unless stated.

The floor holds in three regions. Net carbon growth at the floor exceeds growth 0.10 below it by 0.22 (0.18 to 0.28) Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in the North, 0.19 (0.13 to 0.29) in the West and 0.08 (0.01 to 0.14) in the Southeast. In the Central region the contrast is 0.00 (−0.06 to 0.06), and at top heights of 4 to 15 m the Central floor is contradicted: growth is higher below it.

The ceiling depends on the region and on stand height. In the West net growth peaks at RD 0.38 (0.35 to 0.76) and falls by 0.27 (0.19 to 0.35) Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ 0.10 above the peak, a clear ceiling. In the North the peak is at 0.58 (0.53 to 0.98) and the decline 0.10 above it is small, 0.03 (0.02 to 0.12), testable in 68% of replicates. In the Central and Southeast regions net growth at the reference height keeps rising to the edge of the data (0.88 and 0.99), so no ceiling can be tested; holding stands at the floor there would forgo 35% (26 to 49%) and 49% (41 to 54%) of the maximum net growth. Short stands behave differently: at top heights of 4 to 10 m (4 to 8 m in the Southeast) a ceiling is indicated in the Central, Southeast and West regions, with the net peak at the low end of the data, which says that young, short stands grow less as they get denser. The Central region also shows a ceiling in its tallest stands, at 30 m (peak 0.33; net growth 0.18 lower 0.10 above it, 0.02 to 0.50, testable in 87% of replicates), applied in the tool as 0.45. The per height verdicts are in 2026-11-12_rd-dst-ceilings-by-height_DATA.csv and drive both the calculator and the strategic cost.

The annual natural disturbance hazard at the band center is 2.4 to 4.9% and rises only modestly with crowding (Central 2.8% to 3.9% at 0.20 above the band center; North 3.1% to 3.2%).

Forest type curves

Direct net carbon curves were also fitted for each region by forest type group with at least 6,000 intervals (13 groups) over the full height grid, with the same replicate-wise floor and ceiling tests at every height (B = 100; row shuffling identical in all 13). Because these samples are smaller, a type ceiling is used only when it is indicated at two adjacent tested heights. That leaves ceilings for aspen/birch in the North at 15 and 20 m (peaks 0.58 and 0.70), fir/spruce/mountain hemlock in the West from 15 to 30 m and Douglas-fir at 15 and 20 m (both 0.455), and in short stands only for pinyon/juniper (6 to 10 m), loblolly/shortleaf pine and oak/hickory in the Southeast (6 to 8 m) and oak/hickory in the Central region (8 to 10 m). Spruce/fir and maple/beech/birch in the North show no ceiling that holds across adjacent heights. The type curve can differ strongly from the regional one: a 25 m Douglas-fir stand at RD 0.53 has net growth of 0.97 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ on its type curve against −0.24 on the regional West curve, which is dominated by drier types. The calculator uses the type curve and its tests for net growth whenever one exists.

Band rule as applied

The floor is the regional leveling point minus 0.05 (North 0.435, Central 0.350, Southeast 0.335, West 0.355); the leveling points were fitted on interval mean RD, and on start RD the marginal net maxima differ by 0.01 to 0.09 (largest in the Southeast), so the floor is carried as a management choice and its support is reported by height. A ceiling is applied only at heights where the replicate test indicates one, and it is the higher of the band top and the measured peak: for example 0.58 in the North at 18 m, 0.56 at 20 m and 0.535 at 8 m, and 0.455 in the West at 4 to 16 m. The version 1 type ceilings (from a single height at B = 50) are replaced by the per height type tests above.

Strategic view

From the latest visit of each plot, the share below, inside and above the symmetric band (leveling point ±0.05), with Wilson 95% intervals, is kept for continuity with the dashboard: the median hexagon has 27% of plots inside, with an interval about 22 points wide, and 172 hexagons whose mean RD is in the band hold fewer than 30% of plots there, which is why the tool scores shares rather than means. Under the band rule as applied (floor everywhere, ceiling only at heights where one was tested), the share of plots in the acceptable range is 78% in the Southeast, 70% in the Central region, 59% in the North and 48% in the West (63%, 62 to 63%, nationally); 15% of plots sit above a tested ceiling. The expected cost of sitting outside is net growth averaged over the band at each plot’s own top height minus net growth at its RD, counted only below the floor or above a tested ceiling and floored at zero, averaged over plots with a supported estimate (88% of plots; 83% in the West). It is 0.053 (0.043 to 0.062) Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ and 0.23 m³ ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in the North, 0.063 (0.049 to 0.078) and 0.17 in the West, 0.011 (0.008 to 0.014) and 0.04 in the Southeast, 0.007 (0.003 to 0.011) and 0.02 in the Central region, and 0.036 (0.031 to 0.042) and 0.12 nationally. The intervals carry all 200 bootstrap curves through the plot level costs and add plot sampling variance; the curve uncertainty dominates. Of 1,266 hexagons, 82 have their cost suppressed because fewer than 70% of their plots could be costed, and 78 of the remainder carry a cost of at least 0.10 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹. Plot latest RD is paired with top height at the start of the latest interval, a small mismatch.

Stand calculator

Input is trees ha⁻¹ and QMD, a tree list (both accept per acre and inch units), or a Maine 10 m map value, with region, forest type group, top height, SDIₘₐₓ and, if thinning, the QMD expected after thinning. RD equals Σ nᵢ(Dᵢ/25.4)¹·⁶⁰⁵ over SDIₘₐₓ. The default SDIₘₐₓ is the regional median for the type group; plot values typically run from about 45% below to 50% above it (60% below to 84% above for spruce/fir), so the calculator shows the RD and status range across the 10th to 90th percentile SDIₘₐₓ. Output is RD, the band at the stand’s height, direct net carbon and volume growth with 95% intervals, gross growth, mortality without natural disturbance (flagged), expected loss to natural disturbance, the annual hazard, and the floor test at the nearest tested height. A thin-to target is given only above a tested ceiling, at the net growth peak for that height kept within the band limits, with the expected change in net growth and the residual SDI and trees ha⁻¹ at the post-thin QMD. Above the band without a tested ceiling, the calculator says no density target is supported and gives the change in net growth from thinning to the floor, unless the stand’s own upper 95% bound is below the band mean, in which case it reports that shortfall and the change from thinning to the band center. Net carbon growth comes from the forest type curve where one exists and from the regional curve otherwise; net volume, gross growth, mortality and disturbance are regional, so net carbon and net volume can disagree in sign for a type such as Douglas-fir. By default the band, ceiling and floor tests are the regional ones; forest type ceilings and tests, which rest on smaller samples, apply only when the user ticks the forest type box. The calculator interpolates on the height grid and, at a grid height, reads that height alone, so a curve’s support at the next lower height does not block an estimate.

The map pre-fill was measured directly at 1,541 held out Maine plot visits on 967 plots with true locations: r = 0.53, raw RMSE 0.164 and bias 0.022, within ±0.05 of the field value 22% of the time and within ±0.10 44%. A cross validated linear calibration (field = −0.054 + 1.05 × map) leaves RMSE at 0.163 and a 95% error bound of 0.31. The map pulls toward the middle: it reads 0.30 high in stands at or below 0.25, 0.20 high at 0.25 to 0.435, and 0.15 low at or above 0.75. When the calibrated map put a plot below, in or above the North band, the field plot was in that class 66%, 17% and 76% of the time. The calculator therefore gives no status or target from a map value; it shows the calibrated value, its interval and these class frequencies, and asks for a cruise.

Validation against thinning trials

The validation was redesigned and rerun with the design fixed before results (2026-11-12_rd-dst-ctrn2-validate.R). Plots were the unit; visits were paired into spans of 4 to 6 yr after thinning or on controls; the response was survivor basal area growth from trees linked across the span (net change including ingrowth as secondary); RD used SDIₘₐₓ 1,132 with 451 and 2,080 as sensitivities; the model was RD class plus log top height, classes fixed in advance around the North band; B = 2,000; the equivalence margin was 10% of the band class mean.

In the CFRU Commercial Thinning Research Network (98 plots, 223 spans), survivor growth in the band (0.435 to 0.535) exceeded the class below by 0.16 (0.03 to 0.29) m² ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹, so the North floor holds there. Above the band the contrasts were −0.09 (−0.25 to 0.05) and −0.04 (−0.17 to 0.09), not distinguishable; the smallest contrast the trial could detect with 80% power was about 0.19, twice the 0.09 margin, so the trial can neither confirm nor rule out a ceiling. In the University of New Brunswick PSP16 partial cut and untreated plots (2,180 plots, 4,817 spans), the floor was not distinguishable and the denser classes grew more survivor basal area than the band (0.06 and 0.25 m² ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹), while net change favored the band over 0.535 to 0.70 by 0.10 (0.03 to 0.16). Verdicts change with SDIₘₐₓ: at 2,080 the CTRN floor contrast reverses. PEF precommercial thinning plots were too few (20) and had no controls. The same pre-registered design was then run unchanged on every other eligible dataset in the build (thinning, precommercial thinning and selection cutting; 2026-11-12_rd-dst-ctrn3-validate.R): Nova Scotia PSP (2,423 plots), Quebec PSP (1,499), Ontario PSP (566), Prince Edward Island PSP (528), Penobscot Experimental Forest CFI (151) and New Brunswick CLI (133). Survivor growth in the band exceeded the class below in Nova Scotia (0.065, 0.053 to 0.078 m² ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹), Penobscot (0.14, 0.08 to 0.19) and Quebec (0.04, 0.00 to 0.07), and was not distinguishable in the other three. In every dataset the denser classes grew more survivor basal area than the band. Net change, which includes mortality and ingrowth, told a different story: the band beat the denser classes in Nova Scotia (by 0.06, 0.03 to 0.10, and 0.12, 0.08 to 0.17) and Prince Edward Island (0.21, 0.07 to 0.35, against RD above 0.70), while the floor was contradicted for net change in Ontario (−0.48) and Quebec (−0.20). The trials thus agree with the engine’s mechanism, crowding adds survivor growth and costs it back in mortality, but they do not give one consistent floor. FIA treatment codes were not used because cutting is not identified as thinning. All of these datasets are northern; none tests the Central, Southeast or West floors. Basal area stands in for carbon, so these are checks of direction, not of the carbon numbers. The thin-to targets remain provisional and untested outside northern forests.

Limitations

Curves come from plots with no harvest between visits and are associations, not thinning responses. RD depends on a modeled plot SDIₘₐₓ whose spread is large, and site productivity was tested only with public, fuzzed coordinates on 77% of plots. Mortality without natural disturbance fails the self-thinning check. Single visit RD carries measurement error. The decomposition differs from the direct fit by 0.04 to 0.11 Mg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ at the reference heights and by up to 0.24 at other West heights. In most of the Central and Southeast data the band is a floor with no tested ceiling, so the tool sets a lower limit there more than a target, although the SDIₘₐₓ and measurement error checks suggest a ceiling may exist that the observed data cannot resolve.
